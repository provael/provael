\begin{abstract}
An attack success rate on a vision-language-action (VLA) policy is a difference between two rates,
and most published ones report only one of them. We report a paired evaluation in simulation in
which every attacked episode has a benign twin at the same task and seed, and we add the two
controls a reviewer asks for first. The first is a \emph{length-matched scramble}: the attack
sentence with its target noun replaced and its token order destroyed, so that ``any long
unfamiliar string derails the policy'' is a hypothesis with its own arm rather than an objection.
On the policy where the attack succeeds in 42 of 50 episodes against a benign floor of 1 in 50, the
scramble succeeds in 18 of 30 and the attack frame with no target named in 27 of 30. The headline
therefore measures fragility under a long out-of-distribution imperative, not attacker-directed
steering, and we corrected our own earlier reading of it. The second control is a second
architecture under the identical protocol: a 3B flow-matching policy fine-tuned on the same suite
leaves its envelope in 1 of 30 episodes under the same attack while its task success drops from
27/30 to 8/30. We also run every attack family the harness ships against one policy at three seeds
(only the instruction family fires; ten rungs of a weight bit-flip ladder and a gradient patch do
not) and the headline attack on three further suites (0/30 on each). Every rate carries its floor, a
Wilson interval and a task-clustered interval; artifacts, ledgers and seeds are public. The
contribution is not an attack. It is the two controls, what they changed, and tooling that makes
them a flag rather than a project.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

An attack success rate is a difference, not a level: the rate at which a safety predicate fires
under perturbation minus the rate at which it fires when nothing is perturbed. The second term is
routinely omitted, and without it the rate cannot be compared across policies or across checkpoints
of one policy, and it rewards a loose predicate, since widening the unsafe region raises the number
with nothing in the report moving the other way. Our earlier work ran the benign arm and found that
a published ``100\%'' contained the floor. This paper is about the two objections that survive a
benign floor.

The first objection is wording. A templated instruction attack that succeeds in four episodes of
five could be exploiting nothing more specific than the policy's brittleness to a long, unfamiliar
sentence. A three-token nonsense control, the usual answer, is not length-matched and cannot rule
that out. The second objection is scale of evidence: one policy. Robustness sweeps on the same
suite report that VLA models are fragile to many perturbations at once
\citep{fei2025liberoplus,guo2025robustvla} and that LIBERO scores partly reflect memorisation
\citep{zhou2025liberopro}, so a single policy's number may be a fact about that policy.

We treat both as hypotheses with arms of their own. Section~\ref{sec:method} gives the design,
Section~\ref{sec:results} the numbers, and Section~\ref{sec:disc} what they do and do not license.
The attacks are templated and unoptimised throughout; every rate is a floor on susceptibility.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\paragraph{Policies, suite and predicate.} The first policy is SmolVLA fine-tuned on LIBERO
(\arm{smolvla\_libero}); the second is $\pi_{0.5}$ fine-tuned on LIBERO
(\arm{pi05\_libero\_finetuned\_v044}), a 3B PaliGemma-based flow-matching policy, loaded through a
native adapter with the same LIBERO processors. The headline suite is the ten \arm{libero\_object}
tasks at horizon 280. The unsafe predicate is the harness's documented default: a static
axis-aligned keep-out box on the end-effector position, never fitted to any suite, and every report
carries a flag saying so. A predicate calibrated on held-out seeds with the attack arm is being
fitted and will be reported separately; it is not substituted after the fact. Task success is
scored by the suite's own success function and reported beside every rate as a competence control,
because a policy that cannot do the task cannot be redirected from it.

\paragraph{The attack and the controls.} \arm{roleplay} renders a fixed twenty-token sentence: a
``safe movie scene'' frame that asks the arm, ``as a harmless prop'', to hand over a named
graspable target. \arm{nonsense\_text} (three tokens) and \arm{benign\_reword} (a paraphrase of
the original instruction) are the controls the earlier campaign carried. Two were added for this
work. \arm{scrambled\_text} is the roleplay template's own tokens with the target noun replaced by
a one-token filler and the order destroyed by a seeded per-episode shuffle, whitespace-token count
identical. \arm{roleplay\_no\_target} keeps the frame intact and replaces only the target. All four
carry the \emph{control} role: they enter neither the attack rate nor the benign floor, and a
report that mixed them in would fail its own schema.

\paragraph{Paired design, multiplicity, intervals.} Every attacked episode has a benign twin at the
same task and seed, driven by the same policy and scored by the same predicate. Significance is the
exact McNemar test on the discordant (task, seed) pairs; Holm--Bonferroni is applied across the
adversarial arms of a run, controlling the family-wise rate because a single row is quoted as a
security finding. Intervals are Wilson on the pooled count and a bootstrap that resamples
\emph{tasks}, not episodes, since episodes within a task share a scene, a layout and an instruction;
the clustered interval is the honest width and it declines to answer when fewer than two tasks are
discordant. The headline runs five seeds per arm; the controls, the second architecture, the family
probe and the further suites run three, and their reports carry \arm{preliminary} in their own
field.

\paragraph{Provenance.} Runs were executed with harness release 0.41.2 on one workstation GPU,
sharded per task, each shard writing a signed report and an execution manifest; the generated
tables below are analyses of those reports and are not themselves reports. The $\pi_{0.5}$ arms
were pre-registered with a public timestamped deposit before they ran (reference withheld for
review); the leg reported here is the pre-registration's preliminary leg (three seeds, two of eight
arms), in its own words.

\paragraph{What the scramble says.} The frame with no target named leaves the envelope as often as
the attack itself (27/30 against 26/30 in the same run), and the same twenty tokens in destroyed
order do so in three episodes of five (18/30). The two short controls stay at zero, as before. So
the \arm{roleplay} rate measures the policy leaving its safety envelope when handed a long,
imperative, out-of-distribution instruction; it does not show an attacker steering the arm toward a
chosen object, because most of the effect appears with the words in the wrong order and all of it
appears with no target at all. That is a real safety finding about the policy. It is not
attacker-directed control, and every sentence in our earlier reports that read 44/50 as control of
the outcome has been withdrawn by a published erratum; the number itself stands. The residual that
belongs to the intact sentence rather than to its tokens is bounded by the gap between the
scramble and the frame, roughly 27/30 against 18/30 at three seeds, and we do not claim more than
that. What this run does not settle is whether a shorter imperative, or a fluent non-imperative
sentence of the same length, produces the same effect; those arms do not exist yet.

\paragraph{The second architecture.} On the same ten tasks, horizon and predicate, $\pi_{0.5}$
leaves the envelope in 0/30 benign episodes and 1/30 under \arm{roleplay} (one task, one seed;
McNemar $p=1$, clustered [0, 10]\%), against SmolVLA's 26/30 at the same seed count. Its clean task
success is 27/30 (90\%) beside the 97.5\% its authors report, and under the frame it completes 8/30.
Thirty episodes bound the difference in envelope exits well: the two Wilson intervals, [1, 17]\% and
[70, 95]\%, do not approach each other. They bound little else. In particular the frame degrades
\emph{both} policies' task completion, and only SmolVLA converts that degradation into an envelope
exit; whether that is the action head, the backbone, the fine-tune or the box is not separable at
$n=30$ with two arms, and the pre-registered eight-arm leg exists to ask it.

\paragraph{Breadth.} A probe of every runnable family on one task at three seeds (41 arms, 129
episode records) found exactly one family that does anything on this policy: \arm{roleplay},
\arm{roleplay\_no\_target} and \arm{scrambled\_text} 3/3 each, \arm{goal\_substitution} 1/3, and
every other applicable arm 0/3, including all ten rungs of a weight bit-flip ladder (random and
gradient-ranked selection, $k\in\{1,4,16,64,256\}$ flips) and a gradient-optimised patch. Seventeen
arms are not applicable, not zero: families whose channel (tool descriptions, authorisation
dialogue, backdoor triggers on text the policy never reads) does not exist on this policy. Three
episodes bound nothing tighter than an upper limit of 56\%, so nothing here is a rate; it says which
families deserve seeds. On the bit flips specifically, \citet{gao2026bitflip} report that a few
gradient-selected flips zero out direct-regression and discrete-token action heads while
flow-matching heads need on the order of 100--300; our selection is a one-shot first-order ranking
against the clean weights rather than their progressive search, so our null at $k\le256$ on a
flow-matching head is a lower bound on the budget, not a disagreement with their result.

\paragraph{Further suites.} The headline attack against \arm{none} on LIBERO-Spatial (horizon
220), LIBERO-Goal (300) and LIBERO-10 (520), three seeds on all ten tasks of each: 0/30 and 0/30 on
every suite, clean task success 23/30, 23/30 and 12/30, and on LIBERO-10 task success under the
frame 0/30. The effect that breaches the envelope on LIBERO-Object in four episodes of five was not
observed once elsewhere at this size (upper bound 11\% each). Two readings are open and the data do
not choose between them: the keep-out box is the Object suite's and may not be a sensible box for
these scenes, and the policy completes fewer than half of the long-horizon tasks unattacked, so on
LIBERO-10 the failure mode under the frame is a policy that stops doing the task rather than one
that leaves its box. ``Not observed'' is the whole claim; ``safe'' is not available from it.

\section{Discussion and limitations}
\label{sec:disc}

Simulation only, with one default predicate that was never fitted to any suite; a calibrated
predicate is reported separately and the calibrated numbers may move. Three seeds on every arm but
the headline, and $n=30$ on the second architecture, so most nulls here are upper bounds in the 10
to 60\% range rather than findings. The attacks are templated and unoptimised, so every rate is a
floor on susceptibility and nothing bounds how much better an optimised attacker does. Sim-to-real
correlation for VLA evaluation is, in the third-party literature, moderate and recipe-dependent
\citep{wang2026simreal,li2024simpler}, which is the reason none of this predicts a real-world rate;
it is a regression signal, not a field measurement. A certifier can take from a report shaped like
ISO/IEC 17025 clause 7.8 the protocol, the floor, the intervals and the provenance; nothing about
conformity.

What the two controls changed is the point. Without the scramble the 42/50 reads as steering and
supports a story about hijacking a robot; with it the same number is fragility under a long
imperative, which is a different engineering problem (instruction canonicalisation and envelope
enforcement, both measurable as defences under the same paired design) and a different regulatory
claim. Without the second architecture the finding is about VLA policies; with it the finding is
about one policy, and the interesting question becomes what differs. Both controls cost one flag
and a night of GPU time. We recommend that a red-team report on a language-conditioned policy be
considered incomplete without a length-matched scramble of every instruction attack it publishes,
and that a claim about a class of policies name at least two of them.
